## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
### The Case for an AI-Powered Operations Platform

DHL's operational success at the P&G Jackson facility depends on a deceptively simple foundation: the consistency, safety, and competence of its forklift operators. Today, that foundation is evaluated informally, rewarded inconsistently, and trained by individuals who may lack the incentive to take the responsibility seriously.

This document outlines a phased, twelve-month roadmap to deploy an AI-powered operations platform that transforms how DHL manages operator development, floor accountability, and facility intelligence — delivering tangible value within the first quarter and scaling into a system that positions DHL Jackson as a national model for technology-driven warehouse operations.

### The Three Core Problems This Platform Solves

- **Trainer accountability is broken.** Forklift trainer positions carry a $1–$2 wage premium with no measurable performance standard. Trainers take operators from zero experience to 4–5 classes of equipment — yet no system exists to evaluate whether they are succeeding.

- **Operator behavior is invisible between incidents.** A driver who idles for six hours and rushes the final two may match daily numbers while representing a significant safety risk. That pattern is currently undetectable.

- **Floor knowledge is inaccessible.** Operators rely on memory, peer advice, or physical binders for SOPs. There is no on-demand, role-specific knowledge tool available at the point of need.

## THE CORE ISSUE
### Trainer Accountability: The Heartbeat of Operational Excellence

Of all the challenges this platform addresses, none carries greater long-term consequence than the current state of forklift trainer positions. What happens in the first two weeks of an operator's career does not merely set their habits — it sets the safety culture of the entire floor for years to come.

> *"If the trainers are not held accountable, the operators they produce cannot be either. The standard you walk past is the standard you accept."*

### The Current Reality

Trainer positions currently offer a wage premium of approximately $1.00–$2.00 per hour. This signals to the organization that training is a minor add-on, not a critical function. The predictable result is a trainer pool selected by availability and seniority rather than teaching ability, safety record, or operational excellence — with no mechanism to detect the consequences until an incident occurs.

### What Must Change

**Trainer compensation must reflect trainer responsibility.**
A $4.00/hr premium positions the role as a career milestone. It signals organizational investment in the people responsible for safety culture.

**Trainer eligibility must be performance-gated.**
Only operators who have demonstrated sustained excellence — measured by safety record, consistency scores, and equipment proficiency — should qualify. The role must be earned.

**Trainer performance must be measured by student outcomes.**
Effectiveness should be evaluated by how certified operators perform at 30, 60, and 90 days post-certification. If a trainer's graduates consistently underperform, that signal must become visible to leadership.

**The trainer role must become a career inflection point.**
When trainer positions are prestigious and well-compensated, the best operators pursue them — compounding quality across every operator they produce.

### Trainer Program: Before vs. After

| DIMENSION | CURRENT STATE | PROPOSED STATE |
|---|---|---|
| Wage Premium | $1–$2/hr | $4.00/hr |
| Selection Criteria | Seniority / availability | Performance score threshold |

| Performance Visibility | Invisible | Dashboard — ranked & reviewed quarterly |
| Career Significance | Minor add-on | Gated career milestone |

## OPERATOR DEVELOPMENT
### The Operator Tier System: Measuring What Actually Matters

A trainer program is only as strong as the measurement system that feeds it. The proposed tier system creates a continuous, data-driven evaluation framework that makes excellence visible, rewards it meaningfully, and gives leadership the intelligence to make promotion and compensation decisions based on evidence rather than impression.

### The Behavioral Problem: Rush Patterns

Current shift metrics capture total output — pallets moved, scans logged — but not the distribution of activity across a shift. An operator who idles for six hours and rushes the final two may match daily numbers while representing a significantly higher safety risk. The tier system evaluates **consistency** as the primary safety signal. A consistently paced operator is a safe operator. A spike-heavy operator is a risk that is currently invisible to leadership.

### Tier Structure

| TIER | DESIGNATION | CRITERIA | BENEFITS |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | Provisional Operator | 0–90 days post-cert, building baseline | Standard rate, mentored shifts |
| 3 | Certified Operator | Safety review passed, baseline consistency score | Standard rate, full position eligibility |
| 2 | Proficient Operator | High consistency score, zero incident record | Wage bump eligible, recognition priority |
| 1 | Master Operator | Elite score, sustained record, trainer-eligible | Max wage band, bonus pool, first pick on openings |

Tier movement — up or down — is system-suggested and supervisor-confirmed. No operator is demoted or promoted without a human safety supervisor reviewing the supporting data and conducting a formal evaluation. The AI recommends; leadership decides.

## IMPLEMENTATION ROADMAP
### Twelve-Month Deployment Plan

The roadmap delivers visible, credible results quickly — earning trust with leadership before requesting deeper system access or investment. Each phase builds on the last and functions as a standalone win even if subsequent phases are delayed.

---

### PHASE 1 — Foundation Demo `Months 1–3`

- Build TinyTERM-styled AI operations dashboard — familiar to every operator on the floor
- Deploy AI chat agent trained on facility SOPs, filtered by subsite and shift role
- Implement operator metrics mockup with consistency heatmap visualization
- Demonstrate the idle-then-rush behavioral pattern to leadership with scenario data
- Deploy live to web — accessible from any tablet, laptop, or presentation screen
- Deliver first leadership pitch: the problem, the solution, and the upgrade path

---

### PHASE 2 — Operator Scorecard V1 `Months 4–6`

- Implement grab-drop event logging system for raw material staging positions
- Build consistency scoring algorithm cross-referenced against AGV pickup rate
- Shift timeline heatmap goes live with real operator data
- Begin formal documentation of tier system design and scoring thresholds
- Pilot with small operator group — establish baseline data
- Second leadership review: live data, early scoring results, tier system proposal

---

### PHASE 3 — Trainer System & Tier Engine `Months 7–9`

- Launch formal operator tier scoring matrix (software metrics, no new hardware)
- Build supervisor evaluation trigger — AI suggests, human confirms
- Trainer eligibility gate goes live — only Tier 1 operators qualify
- Student outcome tracking begins — 30/60/90 day post-certification logging
- Trainer ranking dashboard visible to leadership
- Wage structure proposal submitted: $4.00 trainer premium with accountability

---

### PHASE 4 — AGV Intelligence & Full Platform `Months 10–12`

- AGV move bid traffic coordination system — concept demo and architecture proposal
- AGV camera + AI vision prototype: staging verification with RDT operator alerts
- GX pallet lane ratio tracker: per-employee hourly efficiency cross-reference
- Full employee performance database — feeds raises, bonuses, recognition programs
- Third leadership pitch: full platform vision, ROI case, national replication proposal
- Begin conversations about RTCIS integration and formal technology partnership

## BUSINESS CASE
### Return on Investment & Strategic Value

The financial case is built on three pillars: incident reduction, retention improvement, and operational consistency. Each has direct cost implications that significantly outweigh the investment required to build and operate the system.

| VALUE DRIVER | MECHANISM | EXPECTED OUTCOME |
|---|---|---|
| Incident Reduction | Consistency scoring identifies at-risk operators before incidents occur | Reduced workers comp claims, OSHA reporting, equipment damage costs |
| Trainer Quality | Performance-gated trainer pool produces better-trained operators | Lower re-training costs, fewer early-tenure safety events |
| Operator Retention | Visible career path with meaningful tier-based rewards | Reduced turnover, lower recruiting and onboarding costs |
| SOP Compliance | On-demand AI access to procedures reduces non-compliance from ignorance | Fewer procedural errors, faster new-hire ramp time |
| Leadership Intelligence | Real-time dashboards replace guesswork in promotion and assignment decisions | Better decisions, faster, with documented justification |
